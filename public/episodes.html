Flagship investigations

Thirty questions for the future of the free world.

The slate moves from material capability and technological power to institutional legitimacy, strategic dependency, global order and civilizational renewal.

Each investigation is built around a structural trend, a central question and a disagreement serious enough to change what follows.

Episode
The question
01

Can the Free World Still Build?

Housing, grids, transit, factories and defense production increasingly reveal the same failure: wealthy democracies that struggle to execute.

Can open societies recover speed and scale without weakening law, consent or restraint?

02

Who Controls Intelligence?

Frontier AI depends on concentrated chips, compute, models and distribution, giving a small number of firms and states extraordinary power.

Can intelligence remain open without becoming dangerously concentrated or dangerously available?

03

China's Manufacturing State

China has turned supplier density, production learning and industrial scale into strategic power while the West has lost industrial depth.

Can market democracies rebuild strategic industry without becoming command economies?

04

The New Arsenal

Drones, autonomy and the end of expensive war

Cheap drones, autonomous systems and software are rewriting the economics of war faster than Western procurement can adapt.

Can democratic militaries move at software speed while preserving human responsibility?

05

Can Democracy Survive Synthetic Reality?

AI can fabricate any voice, image or document, and make genuine evidence easier to dismiss.

Can societies authenticate reality without creating an authority that decides what is real?

06

Europe After the American Guarantee

Europe has wealth, technology and military experience, but fragmented industry and decades of dependence have limited its strategic agency.

Can Europe defend itself without weakening the alliance that has protected it?

07

AI Needs Electricity

The return of energy abundance

Data centres, industry, electrification and defense production are converging on one constraint: reliable power at enormous scale.

Can the free world build the energy system technological leadership requires?

08

The Demographic Winter

Falling fertility and aging are reshaping growth, public finance, migration, military recruitment and confidence in the future.

Can a civilization remain dynamic when each generation is smaller than the last?

09

Borders, Belonging and Democratic Consent

Immigration can renew aging societies and deepen prosperity, but only where states can control entry, build capacity and sustain belonging.

How open can a society remain without losing the consent that makes openness possible?

10

The Authoritarian Network

China, Russia, Iran and North Korea increasingly reinforce one another across weapons, energy, finance, technology and diplomacy.

Are open societies facing separate adversaries, or a connected strategic system?

11

Can Democracy Act Without a Strongman?

When institutions repeatedly fail to deliver, concentrated power begins to look less like tyranny and more like competence.

Can democracy act decisively without making power harder to restrain or reverse?

12

Can Capitalism Still Legitimate Itself?

Markets remain the engine of prosperity, but scarcity, rent extraction and concentrated ownership are weakening public consent.

Can capitalism recover broad legitimacy without sacrificing dynamism?

13

The Meritocracy Crisis

Open societies need demanding standards, and credible pathways through which talent can rise.

Can merit remain real without becoming inherited, performative or politically allocated?

14

When Intelligence Becomes Cheap

AI and the end of the white-collar bargain

AI may remove the junior work through which professionals learn, advance and build judgment.

What happens to education, work and status when cognitive skill is no longer scarce?

15

The State Can No Longer Afford Itself

Pensions, healthcare, defense, industrial policy, infrastructure and climate adaptation are colliding with debt and weak growth.

Can democracies choose among worthy priorities before crisis chooses for them?

16

Trade After Trust

Export controls, tariffs, subsidies and friend-shoring are replacing a trade system built around efficiency with one organized around security.

What should remain global when economic interdependence can be weaponized?

17

Financial Chokepoints and the Future of Money

Sanctions, reserve currencies, payment rails and stablecoins are turning financial infrastructure into a battlefield of state power.

How long can the open world weaponize its networks before others build credible alternatives?

18

The Elements of Power

Critical minerals and the physical limits of sovereignty

Batteries, grids, chips and weapons depend on mineral processing concentrated in a small number of countries.

Can democracies secure the materials they need without abandoning environmental and labor standards?

19

The Chip Order

Advanced semiconductors rely on a fragile, highly concentrated chain of design tools, equipment, fabrication, packaging and talent.

Can open societies protect a strategic chokepoint without fragmenting the innovation system that created it?

20

The Robot Economy

AI is acquiring a body, bringing automation into factories, logistics, vehicles, care and the home.

Will embodied intelligence restore productivity and industrial capacity, or concentrate power and displace work?

21

Biology Becomes Programmable

Gene editing, protein design and automated laboratories are turning living systems into an engineering medium.

Can open science unlock biology's benefits without making catastrophic capabilities widely accessible?

22

The Surveillance Bargain

Digital identity, biometrics, data brokers and AI administration promise safer, more efficient societies at the cost of deeper visibility into individual life.

How much privacy can an open society surrender before freedom becomes mostly formal?

23

The Digital Sovereignty State

Clouds, data, platforms, models and digital identity are being reorganized around borders and strategic control.

Can democracies reduce technological dependency without closing the internet or expanding state power?

24

The Space Infrastructure of Sovereignty

Communications, navigation, financial timing, intelligence and warfare increasingly depend on privately operated systems in orbit.

Who should control infrastructure on which sovereign states can no longer function without?

25

The New Nuclear Age

More nuclear actors, weaker arms control and the entanglement of AI, cyber and space systems are making deterrence less legible.

Can open societies preserve strategic stability when decision time is shrinking and uncertainty is growing?

26

The World After the UN

Institutions built for 1945 are struggling to act in a world of vetoes, rival blocs and transnational threats.

Should the free world keep reforming universal institutions, or build smaller coalitions that can decide and act?

27

The World That Will Not Choose Sides

India, the Gulf and the rise of strategic middle powers

India, the Gulf, Turkey, Indonesia and other middle powers are pursuing flexible partnerships rather than permanent alignment.

Can open societies build durable partnerships with states that share interests but not all of their values?

28

Climate Adaptation Is State Capacity

Heat, fire, flood, water stress and insurance withdrawal are turning climate risk into a test of housing, infrastructure and public competence.

Can democracies protect people from unavoidable risk without abandoning mitigation or honest pricing?

29

Can Liberalism Produce Loyalty?

Rights and procedures protect pluralism, but may not by themselves generate solidarity, duty or sacrifice.

Can an open society cultivate common purpose without coercion, exclusion or manufactured nationalism?

30

Why the Future Stopped Feeling Better

Advanced societies still invent, but increasingly fail to translate invention into affordable housing, better services, higher productivity and confidence.

What would make progress visible, and believable, again?
