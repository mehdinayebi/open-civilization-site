Open Civilization.

A show by Mehdi Nayebi on the forces shaping the future of the free world in an age of exponential technological change, and on whether open societies can stay strong enough to build, innovate, and defend the freedoms, prosperity, and stability they depend on.

Premise

Free, and fragile.

Free societies are rare. Almost everything we take for granted depends on them: liberty, prosperity, security, open inquiry and the rule of law.

But those conditions do not maintain themselves. Technology is concentrating power. China has turned manufacturing scale into strategic leverage. Alliances no longer look automatic, and international institutions often cannot decide or act.

At home, wealthy democracies remain rich in talent, capital and knowledge, yet often struggle to convert those advantages into housing, energy, infrastructure, industrial capacity and public trust.

Open Civilization examines what makes open societies capable, what causes them to weaken, and how they can renew themselves without importing the coercion of their rivals.

Episodes

The questions that define the show.

The first ten episodes examine the forces deciding whether open societies remain capable, prosperous, secure and free. Each begins with a structural trend, a central question and a serious disagreement about what follows.

Episode
The question
01

Can the Free World Still Build?

Housing, grids, transit, factories and defense production increasingly reveal the same failure: wealthy democracies that struggle to execute.

Can open societies recover speed and scale without weakening law, consent or restraint?

02

Who Controls Intelligence?

Frontier AI depends on concentrated chips, compute, models and distribution, giving a small number of firms and states extraordinary power.

Can intelligence remain open without becoming dangerously concentrated or dangerously available?

03

China's Manufacturing State

China has turned supplier density, production learning and industrial scale into strategic power while the West has lost industrial depth.

Can market democracies rebuild strategic industry without becoming command economies?

04

The New Arsenal

Cheap drones, autonomous systems and software are rewriting the economics of war faster than Western procurement can adapt.

Can democratic militaries move at software speed while preserving human responsibility?

05

Can Democracy Survive Synthetic Reality?

AI can fabricate any voice, image or document, and make genuine evidence easier to dismiss.

Can societies authenticate reality without creating an authority that decides what is real?

06

Europe After the American Guarantee

Europe has wealth, technology and military experience, but fragmented industry and decades of dependence have limited its strategic agency.

Can Europe defend itself without weakening the alliance that has protected it?

07

AI Needs Electricity

Data centres, industry, electrification and defense production are converging on one constraint: reliable power at enormous scale.

Can the free world build the energy system technological leadership requires?

08

The Demographic Winter

Falling fertility and aging are reshaping growth, public finance, migration, military recruitment and confidence in the future.

Can a civilization remain dynamic when each generation is smaller than the last?

09

Borders, Belonging and Democratic Consent

Immigration can renew aging societies and deepen prosperity, but only where states can control entry, build capacity and sustain belonging.

How open can a society remain without losing the consent that makes openness possible?

10

The Authoritarian Network

China, Russia, Iran and North Korea increasingly reinforce one another across weapons, energy, finance, technology and diplomacy.

Are open societies facing separate adversaries, or a connected strategic system?

Principles

Ten principles.

Open Civilization is not neutral about freedom, but it is independent of party and faction. The show tests claims against evidence, history, incentives, power and consequences, and applies the same standard to allies and adversaries. It begins from ten convictions about what allows an open society to endure.

01

Rare, not default.

Most people in history have lived under closed and hierarchical systems. An open society is a historical exception.

02

Dispersed power.

No person, class, company or institution can be trusted with unchecked authority. Power must remain contestable.

03

Liberty as foundation.

Freedom of conscience, speech, association, movement and enterprise makes every other value freely chosen.

04

Revisable belief.

A society that cannot question its assumptions cannot correct its errors or generate new knowledge.

05

Moral universalism.

Some acts remain wrong even when customary, popular or profitable.

06

Markets with rules.

Markets create prosperity and disperse economic power, but require competition, honest prices and correction.

07

Reform institutions, don't destroy them.

Institutions decay and become captured. The first task is to restore function and accountability.

08

Universal values, national communities.

Rights may be universal, but solidarity, obligation and democratic consent usually live within political communities.

09

Tolerance that defends itself.

A free society protects dissent, but need not protect movements operationally committed to ending freedom.

10

Historically conscious.

Open societies should remember how they were built, how they failed and how often progress has reversed.

Host

Mehdi Nayebi.

Born in Tehran and raised in France, Mehdi Nayebi is an entrepreneur and former banker who has built companies across Europe, the Middle East and North America.

He began his career in structured products at Deutsche Bank and Bank of America, then co-founded Alopeyk, one of Iran's largest technology platforms, before building technology and logistics companies in Canada.

Building under an authoritarian system, and later leaving Iran for safety reasons, gave him a direct view of coercion, institutional failure and what closed societies do to truth, enterprise and ordinary life.

Open Civilization grows out of that experience: an inquiry into how free societies can remain open, capable and strong enough to endure.
